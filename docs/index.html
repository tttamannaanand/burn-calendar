Twenty-two years of fire, on one consistent scale

NASA satellites have counted fire hot spots since 2000, but with two different instruments, and the newer one sees far more. We merge the two records so you can compare any week, in any place, from 2003 to 2024.

How it works

  1. Collect

    Fire hot spots from NASA FIRMS: MODIS on the Terra and Aqua satellites from 2003, and VIIRS on Suomi-NPP from 2012.

  2. Grid

    Count the detections in half-degree cells, week by week. Only presumed vegetation fires are kept.

  3. Harmonize

    Match the MODIS counts to the VIIRS scale using the years both instruments flew, one season at a time.

  4. Check

    Fit on 2012 to 2018, then score on 2019 to 2024, years the method never saw, against real VIIRS.

What to keep in mind

  • Three test regions, not the whole planet.
  • Hot-spot counts only. Fire intensity (FRP) is not merged yet.
  • Counts are detections from space, not burned area. Clouds and dense smoke can hide fires.
  • California reads about 12% low in the held-out years, mostly in the extreme 2020 and 2021 seasons.
